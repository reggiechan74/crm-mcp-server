#### Supporting Them Through Biased Thinking

> **APPROACH:** The goal is never to "fix" them or prove them wrong. It's to help them access their own wisdom when their thinking is clouded.

**What Works:**
| Approach | Why It Helps | Example |
|----------|--------------|---------|
| **Curiosity over correction** | Feels supportive, not critical | "I wonder if there's another way to look at this?" |
| **Validation first** | They feel heard before challenged | "That sounds really hard. I can see why you'd think that." |
| **Gentle questions** | Invites self-reflection | "What evidence do you have? What might you be missing?" |
| **Offering perspective** | Expands their view | "I see it a bit differently - can I share?" |
| **Normalizing** | Reduces shame | "Everyone thinks this way sometimes, especially under stress." |

**What to Avoid:**
| Approach | Why It Backfires |
|----------|------------------|
| **"You're being irrational"** | Shaming, creates defensiveness |
| **Dismissing feelings** | "Don't worry about it" invalidates their experience |
| **Lecturing** | Triggers resistance, not reflection |
| **Proving them wrong** | Feels like attack, not support |
| **Fixing immediately** | Sometimes they need to be heard first |

**Timing Matters:**
